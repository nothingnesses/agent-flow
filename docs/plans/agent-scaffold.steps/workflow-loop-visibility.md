### `workflow-loop-visibility`: extend the typed phase carrier and project every active unit while selecting one action (`Q-82`)

Q-82 chose `Schedule visibility and scheduling in stages`. This step is the first visibility stage only: repair phase correctness and plural visibility. It gives `next` no authority to schedule parallel work and does not build the Stage-2 typed fleet. It follows `structured-risk-class-source`, which owns every convergence-bearing `[[active_work]]` variant because task plan review plus increment-scoped Roadmap plan/work review need an authoritative declaration and exact phase-bearing open-loop identity before round one, while exact-step plan review is bounded non-active history. This step extends that carrier rather than replacing its task/phase or step/increment selector.

THE PROBLEM. Stage 1 deliberately selects one lowest-order `in-progress` step and models it as a review loop. At the Q-82 intake, a run therefore presented an exploration as `awaiting-first-review`, hid other active work, and surrounded one selected block with roughly 728 KB of output (the exact historical measurement has one source in `docs/plans/code-value-audit.explorations/Q-58-ablation-design.md`). Status alone cannot distinguish exploration, implementation, and work review, while a step-only phase field cannot represent task plan review, acceptance, or an exploration that deliberately precedes any Roadmap step. The committed log also proves that a plan-review loop may have an exact Roadmap step or increment identity without becoming work review. Replacing the old selector wholesale would erase its legal `ready-to-plan` and `blocked` advice for a fresh or dependency-blocked plan.

THE STRUCTURED CARRIER. Extend the ordered `[[active_work]]` array in the TOML plan source and keep each row parsed at the boundary as an internally tagged union, not as a phase string plus optional fields:

- `scope = "task"` carries `task` plus one of `plan_review`, `acceptance`, or `review`. The task id must equal `[meta].title` or resolve through the plan's declared orphan-task registry. A `plan_review` row must resolve the exact task declaration established by `structured-risk-class-source`; `acceptance` and `review` are single-pass rows and must not borrow that convergence class. Task rows carry no `step`, `question`, or `increment` and are independent of every implementation step's Roadmap status.
- `scope = "question"` carries a resolving `question` id plus `exploration`. The question must have status `exploring`; the row carries no `task`, `step`, or `increment` and is independent of every Roadmap status. This is the legal carrier for Q-68/Q-69-shaped design passes whose implementation step does not exist yet.
- `scope = "step"`, `phase = "plan_review"` carries a resolving Roadmap `step` plus one exact increment owned by that step and declared for `plan_review`. This row is independent of implementation status and never edits it: it represents plan content attached to that increment. It cannot carry task/question identity, omit the increment, select migration-only exact-step history, or become work review by implication.
- `scope = "step"` with `exploration`, `implementation`, or `work_review` carries a resolving Roadmap `step` and exact increment owned by that step. The referenced step must be `in-progress`, and its increment must be declared for `work_review` because these are delivery phases. The `(step, increment, work_review)` key is the durable work-loop selection introduced by `structured-risk-class-source`; it persists across `implementation -> work_review`, and work review reads that exact declaration before any round. These rows carry no `task` or `question`.

Serde/source types use separate task-, question-, step-plan-review-, and step-delivery-phase variants under `ActiveWork::{Task, Question, Step}` with phase-specific payload structs, so required/forbidden fields are represented by type rather than optional cross-products. Validation rejects every cross-product error, unresolved/ambiguous identity, duplicate phase-bearing active identity, non-`exploring` question, task/step plan review with no matching live declaration, increment not owned by the named step, and a delivery row whose Roadmap step is not `in-progress`. A task plan review or acceptance is never represented by changing implementation status, a Roadmap plan review never enters `orphan_tasks`, selects exact-step history, or becomes work review, question exploration never borrows an unrelated step, and no consumer guesses an open increment or phase from declarations/rounds.

AUTHORITY AND LEGAL TRANSITIONS. The orchestrator is the sole actor authorised to advance `[[active_work]]`, as integration-level workflow state after role output and checkpoint evidence are durable; planners, implementers, reviewers, triagers, and explorers do not self-advance it. Update the canonical authority list and orchestrator prompt with that one operation rather than leaving an unowned mutable field. The allowed graph is explicit: task and increment-scoped Roadmap `plan_review` remain on the same exact identity while planner fixes/reviews repeat and retire on convergence; question `exploration` retires after its design result is durable and the question moves to `open`; step `exploration` retires after its result and human decision are durably folded; step `implementation -> work_review` retains exact `(step, increment)`, `work_review -> implementation` on valid findings retains it, and `work_review -> retired` when convergence permits the orchestrator's separate status-complete operation; task `acceptance -> retired` on acceptance or routes to task/increment-scoped Roadmap `plan_review` or the exact step `implementation` unit for a valid shortfall before a later fresh acceptance; task `review` is a single reviewers-then-triager pass and then retires. Opening an entry, changing its phase, retiring it, and reordering active entries are orchestrator operations recorded in the same commit as the boundary they reflect. The tool remains read-only.

THE PROJECTION AND COMPATIBILITY FALLBACK. `next` maps every explicit carrier row to one phase-correct typed `active_units` entry in declaration order. Task and increment-scoped Roadmap plan review reconstruct convergence from exact declaration and phase; task single passes construct no risk/streak/cap state; question and step exploration emit explorer actions without review arithmetic; implementation emits its implementer action while retaining the chosen increment; work review joins only its exact phase-bearing round evidence and selected increment declaration. `selected_action` is copied byte-for-byte from the first actionable explicit unit. The legacy singular `active_loop` remains during the compatibility window as an alias of that selected unit only when it is a convergence review loop; it is never reconstructed separately.

An absent or empty explicit carrier remains legal. Until Stage 3 replaces it, `next` retains the current pending-step fallback only for the two legal pending states: choose the lowest-order `not-started`/`next` step whose blockers are satisfied as typed `ready-to-plan`, else the lowest-order pending step as typed `blocked`. The fallback is inserted into the same projected `active_units`/`selected_action` model with an origin that distinguishes it from an authored carrier row, and appears identically on human and JSON surfaces. It never infers an in-progress review loop, plan-review owner, increment, or phase, so it cannot recreate either the motivating phase bug or a declaration-order/latest-round guess. An empty carrier with legal pending work therefore cannot mean "no action"; an empty carrier with only unrepresented active work reports a typed missing-active-work reason.

Declaration order supplies deterministic one-action focus only, not permission to spawn every visible unit. `workflow-driver-typed-fleet` consumes all units and exact phase-bearing identities without a second selector. `workflow-ready-frontier-scheduler` later replaces only the pending fallback with its ready frontier while preserving explicit active-unit precedence.
