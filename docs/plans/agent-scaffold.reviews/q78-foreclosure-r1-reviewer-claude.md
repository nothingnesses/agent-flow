### PE1C-1 -- `next`'s every-state rule is still unrefused for every state whose context arm is empty

- Owner: `step-intent-encoding-inc1`
- Severity: `medium`
- Proposed class: 1 (a wrong implementation passes while violating a stated rule and the increment's instruction-surface risk ground)

**The rule.** `:112` requires `build_context` to insert a `problem` slot and an `approach` slot "in every loop state". `:61` grounds the increment's `risky` class partly on `next`'s output being an instruction rather than a report.

One is the `beta` fixture body at `:255`, which matches only the ordinary word "blocked". The four real hits name exactly two state families: `AwaitingFirstReview` at `:112` and `:260`, `awaiting-fixes` at `:294`, and `AwaitingFirstReview | AwaitingReviewers` inside the red control at `:318`. No criterion in increment 1 reaches `ready-to-plan`, `blocked`, `converged`, `escalate` or `risk-class-conflict`.

**Why that is exactly the wrong three.** `build_context` (`src/next.rs:993-1021`) has three non-empty match arms and one empty one: `AwaitingFirstReview | AwaitingReviewers` (`:1004`), `AwaitingFixes` (`:1008`), `Blocked` (`:1011`), and `ReadyToPlan | Converged | Escalate | RiskClassConflict | Done => {}` (`:1014-1018`). The three states criterion 7 now pins are precisely the states that already receive an extra slot. **Reproduced demonstration.** I copied the tree into the authorised scratch directory and applied that construction, inserting a fixed `problem` and `approach` into the `AwaitingFirstReview | AwaitingReviewers` and `AwaitingFixes` arms only, then built with the project toolchain and ran the four state fixtures:

### PE1C-2 -- criterion 7 pins two context values to strings `build_context` cannot produce

- Owner: `step-intent-encoding-inc1`
- Severity: `medium`
- Proposed class: 2 (a criterion that refuses a correct implementation)

**The claim under test.** `:264` says the review-state test "fixes the four non-intent values to `worktree`, `ledger.md`, `reviews/reviewer.md` and `reviews/triage.md`, so no placeholder or path variability sits inside the comparison", and the complete expected human blocks embed those literals at `:273-274` and `:288-289`. **Why two of the four are unreachable.** `isolation_tier` and `ledger` are settable: `next --isolation-tier worktree --ledger-fragment ledger.md` produces exactly `worktree` and `ledger.md`. `review_findings` and `triage_findings` are not settable at all. `build_context` computes them at `src/next.rs:1001-1002` from `findings_naming::review_findings_path(context.task, &facts.step)` and `triage_findings_path(...)`, which join a CONSTANT directory template, `docs/plans/<task>.reviews` (`src/findings_naming.rs:31`), to `<step>-<role>-<disambiguator>.md` (`:37`) and `<step>-triage.md` (`:40`). No CLI flag exists for either; `next --help` offers `--plan`, `--source`, `--metrics`, `--ledger-fragment`, `--isolation-tier` and `--json` and nothing else. Because the `docs/plans/` prefix is a literal constant, no choice of task or step can make either value equal the pinned string.

**Reproduced, with both flags applied, on the two states criterion 7 names:**

```text

afx  {"isolation_tier":"worktree","ledger":"ledger.md","triage_findings":"docs/plans/t.reviews/a-triage.md"}
```

The human form prints the same two values verbatim under `context:`. An implementer who writes the expected blocks as the criterion states them gets a byte-for-byte failure from a CORRECT implementation. The criterion's own justification is also false of the real value in the review state: `review_findings` contains the literal placeholder `<disambiguator>`, which is precisely the "placeholder ... inside the comparison" the sentence claims to have removed.

**Why this matters beyond a wrong literal.** The comparison is a COMPLETE-range and complete-key-set assertion, and that completeness is the whole of what the I12 repair adds. Faced with a failing complete-range literal the cheapest repair available to an implementer is to relax the comparison to the intent slots alone, which silently discards the five-key assertion the repair exists to install.

**Smallest correction.** Replace both literal pairs with the values the tool actually emits, keeping them fixed by fixing the task and step instead: state that the fixture uses task `t` and step `a`, so the review-state block reads `review_findings: docs/plans/t.reviews/a-reviewer-<disambiguator>.md` and `triage_findings: docs/plans/t.reviews/a-triage.md`, and the `awaiting-fixes` block reads the latter alone. Drop the clause "so no placeholder or path variability sits inside the comparison" and replace it with the reason the values are stable, that both are pure functions of the fixture's task and step.

### PE1C-4 -- the new empty-body oracle pins the both-fields case, so the sub-rule's "at least one field" quantifier is still unrefused

- Owner: `step-intent-encoding-inc1`
- Severity: `low`
- Proposed class: 2 (a second-guard hole)

The sub-rule at `:100` reads "A STEP THAT CARRIES AT LEAST ONE FIELD CONTRIBUTES AN ENTRY EVEN WHEN ITS BODY IS EMPTY." The oracle the I10 fix adds (`:218-232`) constructs a step carrying BOTH fields. Its red mutation restores only the old `body is non-empty` guard.

A renderer guarded by `body non-empty OR (problem present AND approach present)` therefore passes it, and violates the quantifier: a step carrying one field and an empty body contributes nothing.

Nothing else in the increment reaches that construction. Criterion 4 assigns fields to `alpha`, `beta`, `eta` and `gamma` (`:202`), and my independent census of those four fixture sidecars shows every one has a non-empty body after its heading (2, 1, 1 and 1 non-blank lines), so `each_intent_field_projects_once_inside_its_own_step_details` contributes for all four on the body arm alone; the partial fixture `eta` is the only one-field case and it has a body. Criterion 6's ranges also read bodied fixtures.

The impact bound, measured: no step sidecar in the live plan is empty (`empty_sidecars=0` over `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/*.md`), the six batches fill both fields per step, and increment 3 makes both fields required, at which point the `AND` guard becomes equivalent to the correct one. The defect is therefore confined to the optional window and to a step an adopter would have to author with one field and an empty sidecar. That bound is why I propose `low`, and why I place it at class 2 rather than class 1: the sentence it falsifies is a render sub-rule, not one of the numbered RULES, not a stated risk ground and not a cited Principle, which is the line the earlier `GB-8` ruling drew.

**Smallest correction.** Add one sentence to `:218-232`: the same test also asserts that a step carrying `problem` alone with an empty body returns the `## Step Details` heading, `**Problem**` and its quoted value and NO `**Approach**` label, so the contribution guard is disjunctive rather than conjunctive. A one-field TOML step stops being representable at increment 3, so state that this variant is constructed at the renderer seam rather than from a TOML fixture, which is the same shape increment 3 already permits at `:818` for `status_step_preserves_both_partial_states`, and increment 3's retention paragraph then needs no change.
