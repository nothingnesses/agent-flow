## Remaining shortfalls

### A. `medium` - the repaired active-work carrier has no legal representation for an exploration that is not attached to an in-progress Roadmap step, and this plan is in that state twice right now

Success Criterion 40 promises a union "whose task arm legally represents plan review, acceptance and standalone review and whose step arm represents exploration, implementation and work review" (`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.success-criteria.md:40`). The carrier implements exploration ONLY on the step arm, and that arm requires a resolving Roadmap step that "must be `in-progress`" (`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/workflow-loop-visibility.md:10`); the task arm admits exactly `plan_review`, `acceptance` or `review` (`:9`). Criterion 1 makes the constraint hard, rejecting "a step-scoped row whose Roadmap step is not `in-progress`", and criterion 2 constructs exploration only "as step-scoped units on legal `in-progress` steps" (`:24`, `:25`).

But `AGENTS.md` makes design-space exploration a QUESTION-scoped entry mode, not a step-scoped one: the orchestrator "records the question as an Open-Questions item with status `exploring` ... spawns one or more explorers that write a design-notes artifact" and it "adds no new phase or role" (`AGENTS.md:45`), and those explorers write under `docs/plans/<task>.explorations/`, a task-scoped path (`AGENTS.md:65`). This repository's own history shows the task-scoped case is real, not theoretical: `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.explorations/` holds `Q-59-data-model.md`, `Q-59-command-surface.md`, `Q-59-yagni.md` and five `Q-64-*.md` explorer files under `[meta].title` itself.

The live plan holds two questions in exactly the unrepresentable state. `Q-69` is `exploring` with an owed design pass and states of itself "NOT DECIDED, and NO STEP YET, deliberately. The design pass defines what gets built, and the step is authored afterwards" (`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.plan.toml:2263`), recording that an earlier draft step was REMOVED for depending on an undecided outcome. `Q-68` is `exploring` in the same shape, which `Q-69`'s own ask names ("which is the same shape `Q-68` uses"). Reproduce the absence of an owner: `grep -n 'Q-68\|Q-69' docs/plans/agent-scaffold.plan.toml` returns no hit before line 2200, so no `[[step]]` block folds or claims either question.

The consequence is the defect this step exists to repair. When either owed design pass runs, the orchestrator can only (a) leave the exploration out of `[[active_work]]`, in which case `next` projects no unit for live work and Success Criterion 40's "projects every active unit deterministically" is false in the exact hidden-active-work way the step's own problem statement names (`workflow-loop-visibility.md:5`, `:12`), or (b) flip an unrelated Roadmap step to `in-progress` to obtain a carrier, which is the "fake a phase through a status change" move the same criterion forbids. This is R2-G1's defect class surviving one arm of the repair: the pass-2 report raised only the plan-review and acceptance arms (`q78-acceptance-r2-reviewer-gpt.md:13-15`), so the exploration arm was never adjudicated and this is not a re-raise of a settled finding.

REMEDY, without expanding scope: admit `exploration` on the task arm (its identity resolving to `[meta].title` or the orphan-task registry exactly as the other task phases already do, with the Open-Questions id it settles as the unit identity), or state explicitly and testably that an exploration is representable only once a planner has authored its step and the orchestrator has moved that step to `in-progress`, and then reconcile `AGENTS.md:45`/`:65` and Success Criterion 40 with that narrower promise. Either way criterion 2 needs a fixture for the chosen legal shape, and a red fixture for the other.

### B. `low` - the retained ablation design still prescribes the confidence-interval-overlap inference that the same document's new protocol section and the owning step both forbid

The repair added, to section 4, "Interval overlap alone is not evidence of equivalence or non-inferiority" (`docs/plans/code-value-audit.explorations/Q-58-ablation-design.md:88`) and rewrote the knee reading to "Interpret that delta through the frozen interval/test, not by confidence-interval overlap" (`:95`). The owning step repeats it as a pass condition: "Interval overlap alone never establishes equivalence/non-inferiority" (`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/q58-output-ablation.md:21`).

Section 6 was not reconciled and still instructs the opposite: "Agent variance. Consuming agents are stochastic; a single trial per cell is noise. Mitigations: N replicates, confidence intervals, CI-overlap for the knee rather than point estimates." (`:116`). `CI-overlap for the knee` is precisely the rejected inference, stated as the prescribed mitigation.

This matters because the step forbids substituting a second scoring design and directs the run at this document as its single input: "Run `docs/plans/code-value-audit.explorations/Q-58-ablation-design.md`; do not replace it with a second variant or scoring design here" (`q58-output-ablation.md:5`). A run author following section 6 writes a protocol whose knee rule is interval overlap, which criterion 5 then fails after the trials are already spent, and the frozen-protocol contract in Success Criterion 35 ("retained aggregates plus that command mechanically reproduce the plateau and proposed carrier") is not met by an overlap rule. The repair is one sentence: replace the `CI-overlap for the knee` clause with the section-4 frozen interval/test rule, keeping the per-model reporting instruction that follows it, which is independently correct and is the point of that bullet.
