## 3. Why the four items are one schema, and the reason the brief did not give

The brief asserted that the four belong together. They do, and the binding reason is mechanical rather than thematic.

Typed umbrella membership requires that `render` emits the group heading and the member headings from data. Today the umbrella prose and the member headings are hand-authored inside the first member's sidecar. So the moment membership becomes typed, the sidecar stops owning the heading. That answers duty (g), which asks whether a sidecar may restate a TOML field, and it answers it as a consequence rather than as a separate ruling. Duty (g) is therefore not independently decidable, and any answer to item 4 decides it.

The second binding is a tension, and the brief did not notice it. Item 1 proposes to add prose fields to `[[step]]`, which makes each block longer. Item 3 proposes that a human reprioritises by a cut and paste of a block, which makes each block's length a cost. The two pull against each other. Section 4 resolves the tension by bounding what the new fields hold.

## 4. Item 1, the encoding

### The failure the encoding must catch

Duty (a) asks which failures each form catches, and it names the important asymmetry: the measured drift is a STALE claim, not an ABSENT one. A presence check catches only absence.

The measured population is 45 sidecars that restate `[[step]].status` in prose, of which 21 now contradict it. A required-section check over a sidecar would have caught none of the 21, because every one of the 21 has the section and states it wrongly.

So the choice is not between three forms of the same guarantee. A schema field and a validated sidecar section catch different things:

- A required schema field makes absence unrepresentable. It does not make a stale statement impossible, because prose in a TOML string goes stale exactly as prose in Markdown does.
- A validated sidecar contract catches absence only, and it leaves the second copy of `slug`, `title` and `status` standing.

Neither form catches staleness. What removes staleness is the removal of the second copy, which is what Principle 8, Structured data first, project for humans, directs. A fact with one home cannot disagree with itself.

### The recommendation

Add two fields to `[[step]]`:

- `problem`, one sentence stating the problem the step addresses.
- `approach`, one sentence stating how the step addresses it.

Both are single-line TOML strings, not `"""` blocks. The bound is deliberate and it is what resolves the tension in section 3. Two single-line fields raise the median block from 13 lines to 15, which leaves the cut-and-paste cost of item 3 substantially unchanged.

Everything else the sidecar carries stays in the sidecar. Duty (f) asks which of the sidecar's content counts as intent, and the answer is: the problem and the approach only. The measured evidence, the alternatives rejected, the scope boundary, the priced cost and the executable acceptance criteria are not intent. They are the record of a decision, and `provenance` already points at the artefacts that justify a step. Two fields plus an unchanged sidecar is a legitimate answer, and this pass states it rather than assumes it.

### Why fields rather than a sidecar contract

Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable, prefers the field: once the field is required, a step with no stated problem does not parse. Principle 8 prefers the field: the intent becomes structured data that `next`, `status` and `render` project, rather than prose that only a human reads. Principle 1, Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff, prefers the field: a sidecar contract adds a content rule to a reader whose contract today is "the file exists", which widens `render`'s job without removing a duplicate.

The cost, stated rather than omitted: two fields carry prose into the structured source, and prose in TOML goes stale like any other prose. The field does not fix that. What the field fixes is the second copy, and section 6 and section 7 remove the rest.

## 5. Item 2, the backfill

The human required, on 2026-08-19, that git history is combed so the information is extracted retroactively for existing steps. A design where the field is mandatory on new steps and empty on old ones does not satisfy that.

### The orchestrator's citation constraint is necessary and not sufficient

The orchestrator proposed that every extracted claim names the commit it came from, and that the citation is mechanically checkable against that commit. Accept the first half. Reject the framing of the second half, for two measured reasons.

FIRST, THE PROJECT'S OWN FAILURE MODE IS NOT A FABRICATED CITATION. The `workflow-enforcement-tier-endproperty-fold` waiver note records the diagnosis in the plan already: of the sites that failed review, the ones that failed shared one property, and that property was a claim STATED MORE GENERALLY THAN WHAT WAS MEASURED. A citation check passes on such a claim. The commit exists, the claim points at it, and the claim is still wrong. So a citation constraint alone buys less than it appears to buy.

SECOND, THE VALIDATOR CANNOT RESOLVE A CITATION. `src/plan/source.rs` states the rule in its own provenance comment: `commits` and `findings` are external references and are SHAPE-checked only, because validation is a pure function over the string that never git-resolves a hash and never stats a path. A resolve check therefore cannot live in `validate` without overturning that stance. It belongs in `.agents/checks.toml`, as a command, or in an advisory subcommand of the `audit` kind.

### What makes the backfill trustworthy

Four rules, in the order of how much each buys.

1. TRANSCRIBE BEFORE YOU PARAPHRASE. The problem and the approach for most steps are already written down, in the 2312 lines of sidecar prose, by the person who had the context at the time. The scaffolded template already asks for exactly this text: `pack/plan-template.steps/example-step.md` says "What this step does and how; once done, the outcome and the evidence." So the first source for each step is its own sidecar, and git history supplies the citation for that text rather than a fresh derivation of it. This satisfies the human requirement, because a sidecar line resolves to the commit that authored it, and it removes the paraphrase step for most of the 101.
2. NAME THE SOURCE FOR EVERY STATEMENT. Each backfilled pair carries a source reference of the form `<commit>` or `<commit>:<path>`. Git history is combed for the steps whose sidecar does not state the problem or the approach, and for those the source is the commit or the decision receipt that does.
3. CHECK THE CITATION MECHANICALLY, OUTSIDE THE VALIDATOR. `git cat-file -e` for a commit form, and `git show <commit>:<path>` for a path form. This is a check command, not a `validate` rule, for the reason above.
4. REVIEW IN BATCHES, NOT IN ONE PASS. 101 statements in one increment is the artefact shape this project's review loop punishes hardest, and the plan records why: the diagnosed cause of length in the `workflow-enforcement-tier-fold` loop was a document assembled in layers whose characteristic defect was an aggregate that contradicted a detail elsewhere in the same document.

### How partial adoption stays visible

Duty (b) asks whether a `complete` step owes intent at all, whether partial adoption is coherent, and what makes an un-backfilled step visible rather than silently empty.

A `complete` step owes intent. The reader who asks what a step was for asks it most often about work that is already done, and 68 of the 101 steps are `complete`.

Partial adoption is coherent only as a bounded migration state, and the plan already owns the pattern that bounds it. The recommendation is three increments in one step:

- The fields land as `Option<String>`, so the plan still parses while the backfill runs.
- The backfill fills all 101 in reviewed batches.
- The fields flip to required, and the `Option` goes.

The flip is a one-line schema change whose oracle is `validate`: it passes only when all 101 carry both fields. The window in which absence is legal is one step long, and the end state makes absence unrepresentable (Principle 5). No `[meta]` exemption field is needed, and this pass recommends against one: a declared exemption boundary of the `w4_baseline` kind is right for a rule that will always have exempt members, and wrong for a migration that finishes.

The rejected alternative, stated so it is weighed: land the fields as required and backfill all 101 in the same commit. It has no optional window at all, which Principle 5 likes. It is rejected on the review record above.

## 6. Item 3, ordering and priority

The human decided the direction on 2026-08-19: a separate numeric field for order is the wrong representation, because a reprioritisation renumbers many entries, and a cut and paste of an entry into a different position is quicker. The human clarified that this means a reorder of the SLUGS rather than of the step details, and noted that the step details are a separate artefact.

### The clarification admits two readings

READING ONE, ARRAY POSITION. Delete `order`. The position of a `[[step]]` block in the file is the order. The step details are the sidecar files under `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/`, which are keyed by slug and never move, so the clarification holds as written.

READING TWO, AN EXPLICIT SLUG LIST. Delete `order` and add an ordered list of slugs to `[meta]`. A reprioritisation moves one line. The `[[step]]` blocks stay where they are.

The two differ in cost. Under reading one a reprioritisation moves a block of median 13 lines, or 15 once the two intent fields land, and 89 lines in the worst case. Under reading two it moves one line.

### The recommendation, and the principles that decide it

Recommend READING ONE, array position.

- Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable, decides it. A slug list admits a duplicate entry, a missing entry and an entry that names no step. Array position admits none of the three, because the order simply is the array. A slug list makes the correct state checkable, which is the weaker guarantee Principle 5 explicitly ranks below encoding.
- Principle 2, Minimal by default, agrees. Array position DELETES a field, a sort in `render` and three `min_by_key` calls in `next`. A slug list adds a `[meta]` field, a permutation rule in `validate`, and a lookup on every read.
- Principle 1, Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff, agrees. A slug list introduces a file whose declaration order differs from its plan order, which is a permanent comprehension cost for every future reader.

The evidence that the architecture already leans this way: `steps_from_markdown` (`src/next.rs:560`) already derives `order` from the table index, because the Markdown Roadmap has no order field. So one of the two substrates already treats position as order, and the deletion makes the two substrates agree rather than diverge.

The residual cost is honest and it is stated: a reprioritisation under reading one moves 13 to 15 lines instead of one. This pass judges that a contiguous block move is one editor operation, and that the invariant reading two would add is a permanent cost paid on every read. The human owns this trade-off, and section 10 puts it back to the human as the one place where the recorded clarification does not settle the design on its own.

### The migration has a byte-exact oracle

`render` sorts by `order` then slug (`src/plan/render.rs:177`), and the Roadmap table emits `| Step | Status | Notes |` with no order column. So the rendered document is not affected by the deletion of `order`, provided the declaration sequence equals today's rendered sequence.

It does not, at exactly one place. `rename-to-agent-flow` is declared 84th and renders 98th. So the migration must move that one block from declaration position 84 to declaration position 98, and then the acceptance criterion is that `render --check --strict` passes against the COMMITTED `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.md`, unchanged. A byte-exact oracle covers the whole migration, which is what Principle 6, Ground decisions in evidence, and Principle 7, Reproducible, ask for.

`select_active_loop` is unaffected by construction. It takes the lowest-order in-progress step, which is `workflow-calibration` at order 35 and at position 35 after the move.

A defect found while this was measured, and recorded so the same step fixes it: the doc comment at `src/plan/render.rs:462` describes the Roadmap table as "(slug, status, order)". The table emits slug, status and Notes. The comment is stale today, before any change.

## 7. Item 4, umbrella membership

The orchestrator offered this as a proposal to test. It tested stronger than the orchestrator put it, and the correct form differs from the proposed form.

### Adjacency is already false, not merely fragile

The orchestrator argued that a reorder of slugs would silently falsify an umbrella that asserts "the four steps below". The measurement says the falsification already happened, without any reorder.

Four of the five umbrellas assert adjacency in the words "the N steps below". Two of those four assertions are false on the tree today:

- `convergence-accounting` claims eight steps below. Its members render at positions 13, 14, 15, 16, 20, 23, 24 and 27. Three non-members sit inside that span: `gate-prompt-clarity` at 21, `no-wrap-convention` at 25 and `findings-files` at 26. A second umbrella's members sit inside it as well.
- `file-safety-rules` claims four steps below. Its members render at 17, 18, 19 and 22, split by `gate-prompt-clarity` at 21. Its members are interleaved with the `convergence-accounting` cluster, so two umbrellas overlap.
- `file-safety-rules` also states that `findings-files` is "above". `findings-files` renders at 26 and `file-safety-rules` renders at 17, so `findings-files` is nine rows below.

Two assertions hold: `mode-enum` at 5, 6, 7, 8, and `pack-manifest` at 9, 10, 11. The fifth umbrella, `exploration-mode`, makes no adjacency claim at all, and it is named here because it was checked.

One positional claim in the same set is TRUE and is stated so the sweep is not read as uniform: `convergence-accounting` says `state-schema` follows immediately after `ledger-template`, and `ledger-template` renders at 27 with `state-schema` at 28.

So the count is three false positional claims across two sidecars, live in the plan today. Item 4 is a repair of a measured defect, not a precaution against a hypothetical one (Principle 6).

### The correct form is not the proposed form

The orchestrator proposed that `render` groups by membership rather than by adjacency. Reject that specific mechanism. If `render` gathers scattered members into a group, then array position no longer determines the rendered sequence, which contradicts item 3 in the same schema. The two proposals as put are inconsistent.

The form that satisfies both:

- A `[[umbrella]]` array of tables, each with `slug` and `title`, whose shared context lives in a sidecar at `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.umbrellas/<slug>.md`.
- An `umbrella` field on `[[step]]`, holding an umbrella slug.
- `validate` resolves the reference fail-closed, in the same manner as `blocked_by` and `folds`, and additionally requires that the members of one umbrella are CONTIGUOUS in the step array.
- `render` emits the umbrella title and its context once, before the first member, and emits each member under it.

Contiguity becomes a checked invariant instead of a prose assertion. The umbrella text stops asserting position, because the position is structural, so the three false claims are deleted rather than corrected (Principle 5, Principle 8).

### What that costs, stated because it is the largest cost in this pass

Contiguity does not hold today for two clusters. To make it hold, the 15 steps at rendered positions 13 to 27 are reordered so that both clusters become contiguous.

All 15 are `complete`. Nothing selects them, because `select_active_loop` skips terminal phases, so the reorder is inert for `next` and changes only the reading order of the projected document.

The reorder does not falsify a chronology, and the reason is measured rather than assumed: the plan order is already not the build order. `validation-constraints` is `not-started` and renders at position 96, ahead of `ship-v0-0-2`, which is `complete` and renders at 97. So `order` already expresses intent rather than history, and a reorder of completed steps loses no record.

The human still owns this change, because it changes the reading order of completed work in the published plan document. Section 10 puts it to the human.

The alternative, stated and rejected: allow non-contiguous membership and have `render` emit a back-reference at each later member. It preserves order authority and needs no reorder, and it keeps a split cluster visible rather than hidden. It is rejected under Principle 2, Minimal by default, because it adds render machinery to preserve a state that Principle 5 says must not exist.

## 8. The lettered duties, answered

- (a) THE ENCODING. Two required single-line fields on `[[step]]`, `problem` and `approach`. Which failures each form catches is answered in section 4: a schema field and a sidecar contract both catch absence only, and neither catches staleness. Staleness is removed by the deletion of the second copy, not by a check.
- (b) THE BACKFILL. All 101 owe intent, `complete` steps included. Partial adoption is legal for one step's duration and no longer, under the optional-then-required sequence in section 5.
- (c) THE QUERY. No eighth subcommand. Add `--step <slug>` to `status`, which already carries `--json`, and let it keep the documented best-effort stance of `status` rather than the strict stance of `render`. Separately, and this is where the value is, `next` carries the active step's `problem` and `approach` in its `context` block, because `next` builds the brief an agent is handed. For a step with no intent recorded, `status --step` reports the absence and exits 0, which matches its best-effort contract. After the flip to required, the case is unreachable.
- (d) THE THREE EXISTING READERS. `render` projects `problem` and `approach` into the Step Detail, above the sidecar blob. That is a second copy of a fact ONLY IF the sidecar keeps stating it, so the backfill moves the sentence rather than duplicating it. `next` carries both in `context`. `validate` enforces presence through the required field, and enforces nothing about the content, because content rules over prose are not enforceable and this pass does not pretend otherwise.
- (e) THE ASYMMETRY WITH QUESTIONS. Steps and questions answer "where does the prose live" in opposite ways, and the question half has no drift class of this kind because it holds no second copy. This recommendation moves the step half TOWARDS the question half and does not unify them. It does not unify them because a question's whole body is one field, whereas a step's evidence, alternatives and acceptance criteria are a document. Stating the intent in TOML and the record in the sidecar is the coherent middle, and this pass states the difference rather than leaves it implied. One observation is recorded and NOT decided: the 78 question sidecars are all 0 bytes and exist only to satisfy `render`'s existence check, which is a wart in the reader's contract rather than in this schema.
- (f) WHAT INTENT IS. The problem and the approach, one sentence each. Nothing else. Section 4 gives the reasoning.
- (g) WHETHER A SIDECAR MAY RESTATE A TOML FIELD. No, and section 3 shows this is decided by item 4 rather than independently. Two consequences follow. FIRST, the 24 sidecars that keep an accurate status label carry a second copy of `[[step]].status` with no reader, so they must be swept. The cheapest route is to widen `sidecar-status-opening-drift`, which already opens every affected file, and that widening is a human decision because `Q-78-statusform` set that step's scope. SECOND, the rule belongs in `pack/plan-template.documentation-protocol.md`, because a scaffolded project inherits the same duplication risk and inherits no sentence about it today. It generalises past `status` to the `slug` and `title` that the heading duplicates only once `render` owns the heading, which this pass deliberately does not schedule (section 11).
- (h) THE `next` OBSERVATION. Reproduced. `next --source docs/plans/agent-scaffold.plan.toml` reports `workflow-calibration` as the active loop, `awaiting-first-review`, `rounds 0/5`, and offers a reviewer. The step is in an exploration phase. This design does not fix it, does not depend on it, and does not make it harder: after `order` is deleted, `select_active_loop` returns the same step, because position 35 and order 35 select alike. It stays a separate step and a separate human decision, and this pass declines to bundle it.
