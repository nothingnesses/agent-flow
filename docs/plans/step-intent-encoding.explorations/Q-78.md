## 3. Why the four items are one schema, and the reason the brief did not give

The brief asserted that the four belong together. They do, and the binding reason is mechanical rather than thematic.

Typed umbrella membership requires that `render` emits the group heading and the member headings from data. Today the umbrella prose and the member headings are hand-authored inside the first member's sidecar. So the moment membership becomes typed, the sidecar stops owning the heading. That answers duty (g), which asks whether a sidecar may restate a TOML field, and it answers it as a consequence rather than as a separate ruling. Duty (g) is therefore not independently decidable, and any answer to item 4 decides it.

The second binding is a tension, and the brief did not notice it. Item 1 proposes to add prose fields to `[[step]]`, which makes each block longer. Item 3 proposes that a human reprioritises by a cut and paste of a block, which makes each block's length a cost. The two pull against each other. Section 4 resolves the tension by bounding what the new fields hold.

## 4. Item 1, the encoding

### The failure the encoding must catch

Duty (a) asks which failures each form catches, and it names the important asymmetry: the measured drift is a STALE claim, not an ABSENT one. A presence check catches only absence.

The measured population is 45 sidecars that restate `[[step]].status` in prose, of which 21 now contradict it. A required-section check over a sidecar would have caught none of the 21, because every one of the 21 has the section and states it wrongly.

So the choice is not between three forms of the same guarantee. A schema field and a validated sidecar section catch different things:

- A required schema field makes absence unrepresentable. It does not make a stale statement impossible, because prose in a TOML string goes stale exactly as prose in Markdown does.
- A validated sidecar contract catches absence only, and it leaves the second copy of `slug`, `title` and `status` standing.

Neither form catches staleness. What removes staleness is the removal of the second copy, which is what Principle 8, Structured data first, project for humans, directs. A fact with one home cannot disagree with itself.

### The recommendation

Add two fields to `[[step]]`:

- `problem`, one sentence stating the problem the step addresses.
- `approach`, one sentence stating how the step addresses it.

Both are single-line TOML strings, not `"""` blocks. The bound is deliberate and it is what resolves the tension in section 3. Two single-line fields raise the median block from 13 lines to 15, which leaves the cut-and-paste cost of item 3 substantially unchanged.

THE BOUND IS ENCODED, NOT LEFT AS A CONVENTION. A round 1 reviewer found that nothing enforces it: `deny_unknown_fields` constrains keys and not values, so a later author can write `problem = """..."""` across ten lines and the plan parses. The claim that resolves the pass's central tension must not rest on an author's habit (Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable). So `validate` rejects a newline in either field. The rule is a pure function over the deserialised string, which is exactly the validator's existing stance, and it catches all three ways a newline can arrive: a `"""` block, a `'''` block and a `\n` escape inside a basic string.

A CHARACTER CAP IS THE WRONG RULE AND IS REJECTED, stated because a reviewer proposed one. The quantity section 3 prices is the LINE COUNT of a `[[step]]` block, because block length is the cost of a cut-and-paste reprioritisation. A single-line string of two thousand characters adds exactly one line, so a cap measures something the tension does not depend on, and it fights the one-sentence instruction from the other direction.

Everything else the sidecar carries stays in the sidecar. Duty (f) asks which of the sidecar's content counts as intent, and the answer is: the problem and the approach only. The measured evidence, the alternatives rejected, the scope boundary, the priced cost and the executable acceptance criteria are not intent. They are the record of a decision, and `provenance` already points at the artefacts that justify a step. Two fields plus an unchanged sidecar is a legitimate answer, and this pass states it rather than assumes it.

### Why fields rather than a sidecar contract

Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable, prefers the field: once the field is required, a step with no stated problem does not parse. Principle 8 prefers the field: the intent becomes structured data that `next`, `status` and `render` project, rather than prose that only a human reads. Principle 1, Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff, prefers the field: a sidecar contract adds a content rule to a reader whose contract today is "the file exists", which widens `render`'s job without removing a duplicate.

The cost, stated rather than omitted: two fields carry prose into the structured source, and prose in TOML goes stale like any other prose. The field does not fix that. What the field fixes is the second copy, and section 6 and section 7 remove the rest.

## 5. Item 2, the backfill

The human required, on 2026-08-19, that git history is combed so the information is extracted retroactively for existing steps. A design where the field is mandatory on new steps and empty on old ones does not satisfy that.

### The orchestrator's citation constraint is necessary and not sufficient

The orchestrator proposed that every extracted claim names the commit it came from, and that the citation is mechanically checkable against that commit. Accept the first half. Reject the framing of the second half, for two measured reasons.

FIRST, THE PROJECT'S OWN FAILURE MODE IS NOT A FABRICATED CITATION. The `workflow-enforcement-tier-endproperty-fold` waiver note records the diagnosis in the plan already: of the sites that failed review, the ones that failed shared one property, and that property was a claim STATED MORE GENERALLY THAN WHAT WAS MEASURED. A citation check passes on such a claim. The commit exists, the claim points at it, and the claim is still wrong. So a citation constraint alone buys less than it appears to buy.

SECOND, THE VALIDATOR CANNOT RESOLVE A CITATION. `src/plan/source.rs` states the rule in its own provenance comment: `commits` and `findings` are external references and are SHAPE-checked only, because validation is a pure function over the string that never git-resolves a hash and never stats a path. A resolve check therefore cannot live in `validate` without overturning that stance. It belongs in `.agents/checks.toml`, as a command, or in an advisory subcommand of the `audit` kind.

### What makes the backfill trustworthy

Four rules, in the order of how much each buys.

1. TRANSCRIBE BEFORE YOU PARAPHRASE. The problem and the approach for most steps are already written down, in the sidecar prose, by the person who had the context at the time. The scaffolded template already asks for exactly this text: `pack/plan-template.steps/example-step.md` says "What this step does and how; once done, the outcome and the evidence." So the first source for each step is its own sidecar, and git history supplies the citation for that text rather than a fresh derivation of it. This satisfies the human requirement, because a sidecar line resolves to the commit that authored it.
2. NAME THE SOURCE FOR EVERY STATEMENT, AND MARK HOW IT WAS TAKEN. Each backfilled pair carries a source reference of the form `<commit>` or `<commit>:<path>`, and each field is marked `transcribed` or `paraphrased`. Git history is combed for the steps whose sidecar does not state the problem or the approach, and for those the source is the commit or the decision receipt that does.
3. CHECK THE CITATION MECHANICALLY, OUTSIDE THE VALIDATOR. `git cat-file -e` for a commit form, and `git show <commit>:<path>` for a path form. This is a check command, not a `validate` rule, for the reason above. For a field marked `transcribed`, the check goes further and asserts that `git show <commit>:<path>` CONTAINS the sentence. That turns an existence proof into a relationship proof for the cases where a relationship is claimed. The check is deliberately NOT applied to a `paraphrased` field, because a faithful paraphrase is trimmed or joined and a substring test would fail on correct work.
4. REVIEW IN BOUNDED BATCHES, NOT IN ONE PASS. One statement per step, in a single increment, is the artefact shape this project's review loop punishes hardest. `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.ledger.md` records why, in the paragraph headed "SIX-ROUND SCORECARD FOR THE PLAN FOLD": the diagnosed cause of that loop's length was a document assembled in layers whose characteristic defect was an aggregate that contradicted a detail elsewhere in the same document. So a batch carries a stated maximum of 20 steps, and the boundaries are recorded before the first batch runs.

### Rule 1's yield is UNMEASURED, and this pass says so rather than implying otherwise

Rule 1 is the rule this pass says buys the most, and its supporting claim is that the intent for most steps is already written down. Nothing in section 2 measures how many sidecars yield a transcribable problem sentence and a transcribable approach sentence. The pass's own diagnostic for what fails review in this project is a claim STATED MORE GENERALLY THAN WHAT WAS MEASURED, and the sentence carrying the backfill design commits it. That is stated here rather than repaired by a figure, because the judgement "does this paragraph yield a transcribable sentence" is a reading and not a measurement, so a number produced by one reader now would not reproduce for the next.

What IS measured is that a line count means nothing here. This repository does not hard-wrap prose (`no-wrap-convention`), so a sidecar paragraph is one line and the shortest sidecar in the plan runs to 54 words. Reproduce with:

```
for f in docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/*.md; do printf "%s %s\n" "$(wc -w < $f)" "$f"; done | sort -n | head -1
```

So an argument that a short sidecar cannot hold two separable statements does not follow from its line count. The remedy is a sizing sample, run as the FIRST task of the backfill increment and reported for the two fields separately, because the pass's own reading of the low end suggests the two behave differently: an approach sentence transcribes readily, and a problem sentence is the half that needs paraphrase for a minority of steps. `file-dropper.md`, the shortest sidecar, states an approach and states no problem at all.

### How partial adoption stays visible

Duty (b) asks whether a `complete` step owes intent at all, whether partial adoption is coherent, and what makes an un-backfilled step visible rather than silently empty.

A `complete` step owes intent. The reader who asks what a step was for asks it most often about work that is already done, and most of the plan's steps are `complete`.

Partial adoption is coherent only as a bounded migration state, and the plan already owns the pattern that bounds it. The recommendation is three increments in one step:

- The fields land as `Option<String>`, so the plan still parses while the backfill runs.
- The backfill fills every step in reviewed batches.
- The fields flip to required, and the `Option` goes.

The flip is a one-line schema change whose oracle is `validate`: it passes only when every step in the plan carries both fields. The window in which absence is legal is one step long, and the end state makes absence unrepresentable (Principle 5). No `[meta]` exemption field is needed, and this pass recommends against one: a declared exemption boundary of the `w4_baseline` kind is right for a rule that will always have exempt members, and wrong for a migration that finishes.

The rejected alternative, stated so it is weighed: land the fields as required and backfill every step in the same commit. It has no optional window at all, which Principle 5 likes. It is rejected on the review record above.

## 6. Item 3, ordering and priority

The human decided the direction on 2026-08-19: a separate numeric field for order is the wrong representation, because a reprioritisation renumbers many entries, and a cut and paste of an entry into a different position is quicker. The human clarified that this means a reorder of the SLUGS rather than of the step details, and noted that the step details are a separate artefact.

### The clarification admits two readings

READING ONE, ARRAY POSITION. Delete `order`. The position of a `[[step]]` block in the file is the order. The step details are the sidecar files under `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/`, which are keyed by slug and never move, so the clarification holds as written.

READING TWO, AN EXPLICIT SLUG LIST. Delete `order` and add an ordered list of slugs to `[meta]`. A reprioritisation moves one line. The `[[step]]` blocks stay where they are.

The two differ in cost. Under reading one a reprioritisation moves a block of median 13 lines, or 15 once the two intent fields land, and 89 lines in the worst case. Under reading two it moves one line.

### The recommendation, and the principles that decide it

Recommend READING ONE, array position.

- Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable, decides it. A slug list admits a duplicate entry, a missing entry and an entry that names no step. Array position admits none of the three, because the order simply is the array. A slug list makes the correct state checkable, which is the weaker guarantee Principle 5 explicitly ranks below encoding.
- Principle 2, Minimal by default, agrees. Array position DELETES a field, a sort in `render` and three `min_by_key` calls in `next`. A slug list adds a `[meta]` field, a permutation rule in `validate`, and a lookup on every read.
- Principle 1, Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff, agrees. A slug list introduces a file whose declaration order differs from its plan order, which is a permanent comprehension cost for every future reader.

The evidence that the architecture already leans this way: `steps_from_markdown` (`src/next.rs:560`) already derives `order` from the table index, because the Markdown Roadmap has no order field. So one of the two substrates already treats position as order, and the deletion makes the two substrates agree rather than diverge.

### The project already ruled on this once, and the ruling is now reversed by its own reasoning

This is the strongest evidence available for the recommendation, and the first draft of this pass cited neither side of it (Principle 6, Ground decisions in evidence).

`docs/plans/structured-skeleton.explorations/design-A-minimal-schema.md:83` argued array position, in this repository, before the schema shipped: "Order is ARRAY ORDER: the sequence of `[[step]]` tables IS the Roadmap order. There is deliberately no `order` integer (an explicit index is redundant with array position and is a renumbering-drift hazard; TOML preserves array order, so the array is the single source of sequence)." The same file repeats it as a stated non-goal.

`docs/plans/structured-skeleton.explorations/design-B-rich-schema.md:147` gives the counter-rationale that won: "`order` is an explicit integer, so the render sequences the Roadmap table by it and reordering is a field edit, not a line move. Steps are still identified by stable `slug` (cross-references survive reordering)."

`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/structured-skeleton.md:3` records how design B reached the shipped schema. The human's `Q-46` decision settled the waiver and baseline substrate, "so the target is the full design-B". So the `order` integer arrived as part of a package adopted on a decision about a different sub-question, and design B's own rationale for it is the ONE sentence quoted above.

THE POINT THIS MAKES. The recorded reason for the integer is that reordering must be a field edit rather than a line move. The human's clarification of 2026-08-19 states the opposite preference: the line move is the cheaper operation and the renumbering is the cost. So the pass does not overturn a considered ruling. It records that the ruling's single stated premise is now reversed by the person who owns the trade-off, which leaves design A's argument standing unopposed.

The residual cost is honest and it is stated: a reprioritisation under reading one moves 13 to 15 lines instead of one. This pass judges that a contiguous block move is one editor operation, and that the invariant reading two would add is a permanent cost paid on every read. The human owns this trade-off, and section 11 puts it back to the human as the one place where the recorded clarification does not settle the design on its own.

### The migration has a byte-exact oracle

`render` sorts by `order` then slug (`src/plan/render.rs:177`), and the Roadmap table emits `| Step | Status | Notes |` with no order column. So the rendered document is not affected by the deletion of `order`, provided the declaration sequence equals today's rendered sequence.

It does not, at exactly one place. `rename-to-agent-flow` is declared 84th and renders 98th. So the migration must move that one block from declaration position 84 to declaration position 98, and then the acceptance criterion is that `render --check --strict docs/plans/agent-scaffold.plan.toml` passes against the COMMITTED `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.md`, unchanged. That is what Principle 6, Ground decisions in evidence, and Principle 7, Reproducible, ask for.

THE ORACLE COVERS THE CODE AND THE SCHEMA. IT DOES NOT COVER THE PROSE, AND THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS PASS TREATED IT AS COVERING BOTH. `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.md` inlines every step sidecar and every front sidecar verbatim, and the deletion orphans prose in those sidecars. Correcting that prose CHANGES the projection, so a byte-exact criterion and a prose fix are mutually exclusive inside one commit. Measured:

```

grep -c "order [0-9]" docs/plans/agent-scaffold.ledger.md
sed -n '20p' docs/plans/agent-scaffold.success-criteria.md
```

`success-criteria.md:20` states that the plan TOML "holds the Roadmap (step status, order, dependencies)", and it is a front sidecar, so correcting it fails a byte-exact criterion too.

WHAT THE CITATIONS DO WHEN THEY ARE MERELY REREAD, measured because a reviewer proposed rereading them as positions. Because 84 and 91 are absent, a citation at or below 83 stays exactly correct as a position, a citation of 88 drifts by one, and a citation at or above 92 drifts by two. So a silent reinterpretation leaves some citations right and some wrong, which is worse than either editing them or leaving them plainly stale. Reproduce the cited values with:

SO THE MIGRATION IS TWO COMMITS, NOT ONE. First a code-and-schema commit whose oracle is the unchanged projection. Then a prose-currency commit whose diff to `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.md` is expected, enumerated and reviewed. The step's increments 1 and 2 carry that split.

`select_active_loop` is unaffected by construction. It takes the lowest-order in-progress step, which is `workflow-calibration` at order 35 and at position 35 after the move.

A defect found while this was measured, and recorded so the same step fixes it: the doc comment at `src/plan/render.rs:462` describes the Roadmap table as "(slug, status, order)". The table emits slug, status and Notes. The comment is stale today, before any change.

## 7. Item 4, umbrella membership

The orchestrator offered this as a proposal to test. It tested stronger than the orchestrator put it, and the correct form differs from the proposed form.

### Adjacency is already false, not merely fragile

The orchestrator argued that a reorder of slugs would silently falsify an umbrella that asserts "the four steps below". The measurement says the falsification already happened, without any reorder.

Four of the five umbrellas assert adjacency in the words "the N steps below". Two of those four assertions are false on the tree today:

- `convergence-accounting` claims eight steps below. Its members render at positions 13, 14, 15, 16, 20, 23, 24 and 27. Non-members sit inside that span at 17 (`file-safety-rules`), 18 (`agent-isolation`), 19 (`user-prompts-dir`), 21 (`gate-prompt-clarity`), 22 (`compaction-prep`), 25 (`no-wrap-convention`) and 26 (`findings-files`).
- `file-safety-rules` claims four steps below. Its members render at 17, 18, 19 and 22, split by `gate-prompt-clarity` at 21. `file-safety-rules` and its members at 18, 19 and 22 all sit inside the `convergence-accounting` span, so two umbrellas overlap.
- `file-safety-rules` also states that `findings-files` is "above". `findings-files` renders at 26 and `file-safety-rules` renders at 17, so `findings-files` sits nine rows below.

Two assertions hold: `mode-enum` at 5, 6, 7 and 8, and `pack-manifest` at 9, 10 and 11.

One positional claim in the same set is TRUE and is stated so the sweep is not read as uniform: `convergence-accounting` says `state-schema` follows immediately after `ledger-template`, and `ledger-template` renders at 27 with `state-schema` at 28.

So three false positional claims stand across two sidecars, live in the plan today. Item 4 repairs a measured defect rather than a hypothetical one (Principle 6).

### The fifth umbrella decides the design, and the first draft of this pass did not check it against the right question

`exploration-mode` asserts no adjacency, so the first draft named it as checked and moved on. That answered the wrong question. WHICH UMBRELLAS MAKE A FALSE CLAIM and WHICH UMBRELLAS WOULD VIOLATE A CONTIGUITY RULE are different questions, and `exploration-mode` separates them.

`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/exploration-mode.md:1` names the cluster as `exploration-mode`, `ledger-parse`, `workflow-invariants` and `state-queries`. They render at 37, 40, 41 and 46. Six non-members sit inside that span: `session-preflight` 38, `round-log-core` 39, `escalation-exempt` 42, `decision-receipt` 43, `waiver-model` 44 and `structured-skeleton` 45.

So the plan carries a `complete`, genuine and useful umbrella whose member set is NOT contiguous and whose prose asserts nothing false. That refutes, from the project's own corpus, the premise a contiguity rule rests on. A non-contiguous member set is not an illegal state in this plan. It is what a shared-context grouping looks like once other work lands between its members. An umbrella here means SHARED CONTEXT and not a positional claim, so the illegal state to remove is "prose that asserts a position", and typed membership plus a generated heading removes it whether or not the members sit together.

### The comparison section 6 ran, run again here

Section 6 rejects an explicit slug list on a stated rule: when an ENCODABLE alternative exists, prefer it to a CHECKABLE one, because a checkable guarantee is the weaker one Principle 5 ranks below encoding. The first draft never ran that comparison for the umbrella. It compared a contiguity invariant against render-side back-references, rejected the back-references under Principle 2, and stopped. A reader who takes section 6's rule at face value and reaches section 7 gets no answer to the obvious question. That is a defect in the reasoning whatever the design turns out to be, so the comparison runs here.

THE ENCODABLE FORM OF UMBRELLA MEMBERSHIP IS NESTED GROUPS. One array of groups, each with an optional title and context and its own nested step array, with a run of ungrouped steps as an untitled group. Plan order becomes group order and then within-group order, so a non-contiguous member set is not writable at all.

NESTED GROUPS ARE REJECTED, and the pricing is the reason running the comparison does not rescue the invariant.

- Nested groups do not remove the reorder. They make the reorder MANDATORY. To nest `convergence-accounting`'s members together, those members must become adjacent in plan order, which is exactly the change the first draft asked the human to approve. Under nested groups no alternative is representable.
- Every consumer must flatten: `src/plan/source.rs`, `src/plan/render.rs`, `src/next.rs`, `src/workflow.rs` and `src/metrics.rs`.
- Every fixture and every inline TOML literal in the test suite changes shape.
- The Markdown substrate in `src/plan.rs` owes a parity story that it does not have today.
- Every ungrouped step acquires membership of a synthetic untitled group, which is a construct in the schema that nothing asks for.

So section 7's first conclusion does not fall to section 6's test. It falls to the corpus, because the state the invariant forbids is a state the plan holds today and holds legitimately.

SECTION 6'S ARGUMENT STANDS AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN. The other available repair is to state plainly that a checkable form is acceptable here, and to withdraw the argument used against the slug list. That repair is rejected. The slug-list argument is sound on its own terms, it carries the first decision in section 11, and withdrawing it to buy internal consistency would trade a good argument for a worse design.

### The form this pass recommends

- A `[[umbrella]]` array of tables, each with `slug` and `title`.
- An `umbrella` field on `[[step]]`, holding an umbrella slug. `validate` resolves the reference fail-closed, in the same manner as `blocked_by` and `folds`.
- NO CONTIGUITY RULE. `validate` says nothing about where the members sit.
- `render` emits one group per CONTIGUOUS RUN of the `umbrella` value. The umbrella's title and its shared context render on the FIRST run. A later run of the same umbrella renders the title with a generated continuation marker and no repeated context.
- The shared context lives at `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.umbrellas/<slug>.md` and is OPTIONAL. An absent file means no context paragraph and is not an error.

WHY THE `umbrella` FIELD STAYS, stated because a reviewer proposed deleting it as a second copy of a fact the array already carries. It is not a second copy. `mode-enum` occupies rendered 5 to 8 and `pack-manifest` occupies 9 to 11. Position alone cannot tell a reader where one run ends and the next begins, so two adjacent runs are indistinguishable from one longer run. The field carries the PARTITION, and position does not carry the partition at all. That is the difference from `order`, which WAS fully derivable from declaration position.

WHY ONE GROUP PER RUN RATHER THAN A BACK-REFERENCE. The first draft rejected a variant that keeps one group and emits a pointer at each later member. That rejection stands, under Principle 2: a pointer is render machinery added to describe a split. A group per run needs no pointer and no new schema, because the run boundary is already in the data.

WHY THE CONTEXT SIDECAR IS OPTIONAL. `render_plan` (`src/plan/render.rs:135`) collects every unreadable sidecar and returns an error, so it produces no output at all. Duty (e) records that every question sidecar is 0 bytes and exists only to satisfy that check, and calls it a wart in the reader's contract. A required umbrella sidecar imports the same wart for a third file class, and a scaffolded project declaring an umbrella with no shared context must then create an empty file or get no render. One optional clause avoids that, and it does not require fixing the existence contract for the other two classes, which stays a separate step.

### What the recommended form costs, and what it buys

THE COST. A scattered cluster looks scattered in the rendered document. `convergence-accounting`, `file-safety-rules` and `exploration-mode` each render their title more than once today. Reproduce each umbrella's runs by taking its member list from its sidecar and resolving each slug with the position command in section 2.

This pass judges that cost a FEATURE. A contiguity rule hides the same drift behind a validator error, and the cheapest way for an author to clear that error is to delete the `umbrella` field from the step being moved, which silently destroys the membership fact. A visible continuation marker states the truth. A validator error trains the author to erase it.

THE BUY.

- Array-position authority stays intact, so item 3 and item 4 stop contradicting each other.
- Reprioritisation stays one cut and paste for every step in the plan, which is the human's stated requirement. Under a contiguity rule, cutting a member out of its run makes the plan invalid, and pasting any step into an umbrella's span makes the plan invalid for a reason unrelated to the step being moved. A step's legal positions would depend on other steps' `umbrella` values, which is a non-local constraint over the whole array.
- No illegal state is invented, so Principle 5 is not misapplied in either direction.
- The three false positional claims are deleted rather than corrected, because the position becomes structural (Principle 8).
- The tidy-up of the interleaved clusters becomes an OPTIONAL improvement offered on readability grounds, rather than a precondition of the schema.

## 12. What not to build, and the line that decides it

THE LINE, DRAWN BECAUSE THE FIRST DRAFT REACHED FOR IT AND DID NOT DRAW IT. A check that compares two fields of the same plan for equality is ADMISSIBLE. - ADMITTED. After the backfill, assert that no sidecar contains its own step's `problem` or `approach` string verbatim.
