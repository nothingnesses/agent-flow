## 4. Item 1, the encoding

### The failure the encoding must catch

Duty (a) asks which failures each form catches, and it names the important asymmetry: the measured drift is a STALE claim, not an ABSENT one. A presence check catches only absence.

The measured population is 45 sidecars that restate `[[step]].status` in prose, of which 21 now contradict it. A required-section check over a sidecar would have caught none of the 21, because every one of the 21 has the section and states it wrongly.

So the choice is not between three forms of the same guarantee. A schema field and a validated sidecar section catch different things:

- A required schema field makes absence unrepresentable. It does not make a stale statement impossible, because prose in a TOML string goes stale exactly as prose in Markdown does.
- A validated sidecar contract catches absence only, and it leaves the second copy of `slug`, `title` and `status` standing.

Neither form catches staleness. What removes staleness is the removal of the second copy, which is what Principle 8, Structured data first, project for humans, directs. A fact with one home cannot disagree with itself.

### The recommendation

Add two fields to `[[step]]`:

- `problem`, non-empty prose stating the problem the step addresses, in one or more paragraphs.
- `approach`, non-empty prose stating how the step addresses it, in one or more paragraphs.

A single paragraph is valid. A field may use a single-line basic or literal TOML string, a multiline basic string, a multiline literal string, or escaped newlines inside a basic string. `validate` rejects only absence after the required flip and a value empty after trim. It does not inspect physical TOML representation and does not reject line endings.

THE THREE HUMAN PROJECTIONS PRESERVE THE LOGICAL VALUE UNDER LABELS. `render` inserts `**Problem**` and `**Approach**` blocks after the sidecar's leading heading and prefixes each logical line with a block-quote marker, preserving blank lines as paragraph separators. Human `next` and human `status --step` use lower-case map labels and the same quoted-line shape at their existing indentation. JSON keeps the deserialised string unchanged. The exact bytes and executable fixtures live in `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/step-intent-encoding.md`, increment 1.

THE FORMAT WAS PROVED BEFORE IT WAS SPECIFIED. A small scratch proof using the current crate's `toml = "0.8"` dependency parsed multiline basic and literal strings, escaped-newline values, a continuation-escaped physical multiline value and ordinary single-paragraph strings. The proposed `render`, human and JSON `next`, and human and JSON `status --step` formats all retained the internal blank line; the continuation became one logical paragraph as TOML defines. No projection lacked a paragraph-preserving format, so the design proceeds.

NO SENTENCE, LINE OR CHARACTER CAP IS IMPOSED. Decision 26 accepts the larger TOML block and reorder diff a paragraph value can produce. Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff and Structured data first, project for humans decide the trade: the schema represents the prose the step needs rather than shaping that prose around a file-move micro-cost. Make illegal states unrepresentable still applies to required presence and non-empty-after-trim; it does not turn physical source layout into a second state machine.

Everything else the sidecar carries stays in the sidecar. Duty (f) asks which of the sidecar's content counts as intent, and the answer is: the problem and the approach only. The measured evidence, the alternatives rejected, the scope boundary, the priced cost and the executable acceptance criteria are not intent. They are the record of a decision, and `provenance` already points at the artefacts that justify a step. Two fields plus an unchanged sidecar is a legitimate answer, and this pass states it rather than assumes it.

### Why fields rather than a sidecar contract

Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable, prefers the field: once the field is required, a step with no stated problem does not parse. Principle 8 prefers the field: the intent becomes structured data that `next`, `status` and `render` project, rather than prose that only a human reads. Principle 1, Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff, prefers the field: a sidecar contract adds a content rule to a reader whose contract today is "the file exists", which widens `render`'s job without removing a duplicate.

The cost, stated rather than omitted: two fields carry prose into the structured source, and prose in TOML goes stale like any other prose. The field does not fix that. What the field fixes is the second copy, and section 6 removes the rest.

## 5. Item 2, the backfill

The human required, on 2026-08-19, that git history is combed so the information is extracted retroactively for existing steps. A design where the field is mandatory on new steps and empty on old ones does not satisfy that.

### The orchestrator's citation constraint is necessary and not sufficient

The orchestrator proposed that every extracted claim names the commit it came from, and that the citation is mechanically checkable against that commit. Accept the first half. Reject the framing of the second half, for two measured reasons.

FIRST, THE PROJECT'S OWN FAILURE MODE IS NOT A FABRICATED CITATION. The `workflow-enforcement-tier-endproperty-fold` waiver note records the diagnosis in the plan already: of the sites that failed review, the ones that failed shared one property, and that property was a claim STATED MORE GENERALLY THAN WHAT WAS MEASURED. A citation check passes on such a claim. The commit exists, the claim points at it, and the claim is still wrong. So a citation constraint alone buys less than it appears to buy.

SECOND, THE VALIDATOR CANNOT RESOLVE A CITATION. `src/plan/source.rs` states the rule in its own provenance comment: `commits` and `findings` are external references and are SHAPE-checked only, because validation is a pure function over the string that never git-resolves a hash and never stats a path. A resolve check therefore cannot live in `validate` without overturning that stance. It belongs in `.agents/checks.toml`, as a command, or in an advisory subcommand of the `audit` kind.

### What makes the backfill trustworthy

Four rules, in the order of how much each buys.

1. TRANSCRIBE BEFORE YOU PARAPHRASE. The problem and the approach for most steps are already written down, in the sidecar prose, by the person who had the context at the time. The scaffolded template already asks for exactly this text: `pack/plan-template.steps/example-step.md` says "What this step does and how; once done, the outcome and the evidence." So the first source for each step is its own sidecar, and git history supplies the citation for that text rather than a fresh derivation of it.
2. NAME THE SOURCE FOR EVERY STATEMENT, AND MARK HOW IT WAS TAKEN, IN A RECORD OUTSIDE THE PLAN. A prose source is `<commit>:<path>`, where `<path>` is the path AS OF THAT COMMIT. A decision source is `<commit>:docs/metrics/workflow.jsonl#L<line>`, where the positive one-based line number identifies one exact receipt in that commit; the unqualified metrics log is not admissible. Each field is marked `transcribed` or `paraphrased`. THE BARE `<commit>` FORM IS NOT ADMISSIBLE, and an earlier draft of this sentence offered it: a `transcribed` row citing a bare commit cannot run the substring check that gives the mark its meaning, so the format that cannot express the unprovable row beats a check that hunts for it (Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable). `step-intent-encoding` rule 5 is the authority on the grammar. THOSE TWO FACTS ARE NOT SCHEMA. The human decided on 2026-08-21 that they live in a migration record the migration owns and the migration deletes, over a `[[step]]` sub-table. The reason is this pass's own, at section 11: migration bookkeeping must not outlive the migration, which is the same argument that refuses a `[meta]` exemption field for the same backfill. A second reason is architectural: migration bookkeeping is evidence about the one-time extraction, not enduring step intent, so placing it in every step would make temporary process data permanent. `step-intent-encoding` rule 5 names the record's path, its columns and its source grammar, its six batch increments append to the record and its increment 3 deletes it. Git history is combed for the steps whose sidecar states neither the problem nor the approach, and for those the source is the prose blob or exact decision receipt that does.
3. CHECK THE CITATION MECHANICALLY, OUTSIDE THE VALIDATOR. A prose source resolves with `git show <commit>:<path>`. A decision source strips the `#L<line>` selector for `git show`, reads exactly that line, requires a schema-valid `type:"decision"` object whose `task` equals the migration row's slug, and exposes only its string-valued `chosen`, `recommendation` and `options` as source text. The receipt line is the precise identity and `task == slug` is the bounded relevance oracle; the check never admits the entire metrics log as relevant. This is a check command, not a `validate` rule, for the reason above. For a field marked `transcribed`, the complete value, paragraph boundaries included, must occur in the prose blob or in the selected receipt's decoded strings. WHAT THAT PROVES, STATED EXACTLY, BECAUSE AN EARLIER DRAFT OVERCLAIMED IT: it proves WHICH COMMIT HELD THE PROSE or exact receipt. It does not prove that the value was derived from history rather than moved out of the sidecar this increment edits. THE SOURCE IS THE EARLIEST COMMIT THAT HOLDS THE VALUE OR THE IDENTIFIED RECEIPT, decided by the human on 2026-08-21 (`q_id:"Q-78-earliestcommit"`, section 10 item 9), and `step-intent-encoding` rule 6 is the authority on it. For prose, `git log --reverse --format=%H -S"$value" -- . | head -1` returns the earliest across renames. For a receipt, the needle is the exact selected JSONL line and the path is `docs/metrics/workflow.jsonl`. The step's R2 script prints `NOT THE EARLIEST` when the cited commit differs. MEASURED, the earliest commit for two live sidecar values predates the sidecar tree itself, so the tip does not satisfy the check. WHAT THE EARLIEST-COMMIT RULE ADDS is a date: a value invented during the backfill has the backfill's own commit as its earliest, and a receipt source identifies the commit where the relevant decision record first appeared. What stays open is derivation rather than provenance, and section 12 records it. The check is deliberately NOT applied to a `paraphrased` field, because a faithful paraphrase is trimmed or joined and a substring test would fail on correct work. THE PARAPHRASE ROUTE IS AN OPT-OUT, SO IT IS BOUNDED AND MEASURED: increment 2 reports the transcribed and paraphrased counts per batch, so an implementation that marks every field `paraphrased` is visible rather than silently compliant.
4. REVIEW IN BOUNDED BATCHES, NOT IN ONE PASS. Two prose values per step, each potentially several paragraphs, in a single increment is the artefact shape this project's review loop punishes hardest. `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.ledger.md` records why, in the paragraph headed "SIX-ROUND SCORECARD FOR THE PLAN FOLD": the diagnosed cause of that loop's length was a document assembled in layers whose characteristic defect was an aggregate that contradicted a detail elsewhere in the same document. So a batch carries a stated maximum of 20 steps, and the boundaries are recorded before the first batch runs.

### Rule 1's yield is UNMEASURED, and this pass says so rather than implying otherwise

Rule 1 is the rule this pass says buys the most, and its supporting claim is that the intent for most steps is already written down. Nothing in section 2 measures how many sidecars yield a transcribable problem value and a transcribable approach value. The pass's own diagnostic for what fails review in this project is a claim STATED MORE GENERALLY THAN WHAT WAS MEASURED, and the sentence carrying the backfill design commits it. That is stated here rather than repaired by a figure, because the judgement "does this prose yield a transcribable value" is a reading and not a measurement, so a number produced by one reader now would not reproduce for the next.

What IS measured is that a line count means nothing here. This repository does not hard-wrap prose (`no-wrap-convention`), so a sidecar paragraph is one line and the shortest sidecar in the plan runs to 54 words. Reproduce with:

```
for f in docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/*.md; do printf "%s %s\n" "$(wc -w < $f)" "$f"; done | sort -n | head -1
```

So an argument that a short sidecar cannot hold two separable statements does not follow from its line count. The remedy is a sizing sample, run as the FIRST task of the backfill increment and reported for the two fields separately, because the pass's own reading of the low end suggests the two behave differently: an approach value transcribes readily, and the problem value is the half that needs paraphrase for a minority of steps. `file-dropper.md`, the shortest sidecar, states an approach and states no problem at all. THE SAMPLE IS AN ACCEPTANCE CRITERION AND NOT A PARAGRAPH, because an earlier draft named the remedy and left it unenforced, so an implementer who skipped it satisfied every criterion.

### How partial adoption stays visible

Duty (b) asks whether a `complete` step owes intent at all, whether partial adoption is coherent, and what makes an un-backfilled step visible rather than silently empty.

A `complete` step owes intent. The reader who asks what a step was for asks it most often about work that is already done, and most of the plan's steps are `complete`.

Partial adoption is coherent only as a bounded migration state, and the plan already owns the pattern that bounds it. The recommendation is three phases in one step:

- The fields land as `Option<String>`, so the plan still parses while the backfill runs. One increment.
- The backfill fills every step in reviewed batches. The human split this phase into ONE INCREMENT PER BATCH on 2026-08-21, because six batches inside one loop against a five-round cap guarantees either a forced escalation or a convergence certified while a batch is owed. `step-intent-encoding` declares six.
- The fields flip to required, and the `Option` goes. One increment.

So the step declares eight increments, and an earlier draft of this sentence said three. The flip is a small schema type change whose oracle is `validate`: it passes only when every step in the plan carries both fields. The window in which absence is legal is SIX BATCHES long, and the end state makes absence unrepresentable (Principle 5). No `[meta]` exemption field is needed, and this pass recommends against one: a declared exemption boundary of the `w4_baseline` kind is right for a rule that will always have exempt members, and wrong for a migration that finishes. The migration record in rule 2 is the same argument applied to the same subject, which is why it lives outside the plan and leaves with the migration.

The rejected alternative, stated so it is weighed: land the fields as required and backfill every step in the same commit. It has no optional window at all, which Principle 5 likes. It is rejected on the review record above.

## 6. Item 3, ordering and priority

The human decided the direction on 2026-08-19: a separate numeric field for order is the wrong representation, because a reprioritisation renumbers many entries, and a cut and paste of an entry into a different position is quicker. The human clarified that this means a reorder of the SLUGS rather than of the step details, and noted that the step details are a separate artefact. THE HUMAN THEN CHOSE BETWEEN THE TWO READINGS THAT CLARIFICATION ADMITS, ON 2026-08-21: array position, and `order` is deleted. Receipt `q_id:"Q-78-arrayorder"`.

### The clarification admits two readings

READING ONE, ARRAY POSITION. Delete `order`. The position of a `[[step]]` block in the file is the order. The step details are the sidecar files under `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/`, which are keyed by slug and never move, so the clarification holds as written.

READING TWO, AN EXPLICIT SLUG LIST. Delete `order` and add an ordered list of slugs to `[meta]`. A reprioritisation moves one line. The `[[step]]` blocks stay where they are.

THE TWO DIFFER IN EDITING COST. Under reading one a reprioritisation moves one complete `[[step]]` block. Paragraph intent values can make that block and its diff larger, and decision 26 accepts the cost. Under reading two a reprioritisation moves one slug-list entry but introduces a second ordered structure that can omit, duplicate or invent a member. No physical-line figure is a design constraint or pass condition; the comparison is one block move against one list-entry move plus a permanent permutation invariant.

### The recommendation, and the principles that decide it

Recommend READING ONE, array position. The human took it on 2026-08-21.

- Principle 5, Make illegal states unrepresentable, decides it. A slug list admits a duplicate entry, a missing entry and an entry that names no step. Array position admits none of the three, because the order simply is the array. A slug list makes the correct state checkable, which is the weaker guarantee Principle 5 explicitly ranks below encoding.
- Principle 2, Minimal by default, agrees. Array position DELETES a field, a sort in `render` and three `min_by_key` calls in `next`. A slug list adds a `[meta]` field, a permutation rule in `validate`, and a lookup on every read.
- Principle 1, Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff, agrees. A slug list introduces a file whose declaration order differs from its plan order, which is a permanent comprehension cost for every future reader.

The evidence that the architecture already leans this way: `steps_from_markdown` (`src/next.rs:560`) already derives `order` from the table index, because the Markdown Roadmap has no order field. So one of the two substrates already treats position as order, and the deletion makes the two substrates agree rather than diverge.

### The project already ruled on this once, and the ruling is now reversed by its own reasoning

This is the strongest evidence available for the recommendation, and the first draft of this pass cited neither side of it (Principle 6, Ground decisions in evidence).

`docs/plans/structured-skeleton.explorations/design-A-minimal-schema.md:83` argued array position, in this repository, before the schema shipped: "Order is ARRAY ORDER: the sequence of `[[step]]` tables IS the Roadmap order. There is deliberately no `order` integer (an explicit index is redundant with array position and is a renumbering-drift hazard; TOML preserves array order, so the array is the single source of sequence)." The same file repeats it as a stated non-goal.

`docs/plans/structured-skeleton.explorations/design-B-rich-schema.md:147` gives the counter-rationale that won: "`order` is an explicit integer, so the render sequences the Roadmap table by it and reordering is a field edit, not a line move. Steps are still identified by stable `slug` (cross-references survive reordering)."

`docs/plans/agent-scaffold.steps/structured-skeleton.md:3` records how design B reached the shipped schema. The human's `Q-46` decision settled the waiver and baseline substrate, "so the target is the full design-B". So the `order` integer arrived as part of a package adopted on a decision about a different sub-question, and design B's own rationale for it is the ONE sentence quoted above.

THE POINT THIS MAKES. The recorded reason for the integer is that reordering must be a field edit rather than a line move. The human's clarification of 2026-08-19 states the opposite preference: the line move is the cheaper operation and the renumbering is the cost. So the pass does not overturn a considered ruling. It records that the ruling's single stated premise is now reversed by the person who owns the trade-off, which leaves design A's argument standing unopposed.

The residual cost is honest and it is stated: a reprioritisation under reading one moves a contiguous block instead of one slug-list entry, and paragraph values can enlarge that move. This pass judges that a contiguous block move is one editor operation, and that the invariant reading two would add is a permanent cost paid on every read.

### The migration has a byte-exact oracle

`render` sorts by `order` then slug (`src/plan/render.rs:177`), and the Roadmap table emits `| Step | Status | Notes |` with no order column. So the rendered document is not affected by the deletion of `order`, provided the declaration sequence equals today's rendered sequence.

It does not, at exactly one place. `rename-to-agent-flow` is declared 84th and renders 98th. So the migration must move that one block from declaration position 84 to declaration position 98, and then the acceptance criterion is that `render --check --strict docs/plans/agent-scaffold.plan.toml` passes against the COMMITTED `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.md`, unchanged. That is what Principle 6, Ground decisions in evidence, and Principle 7, Reproducible, ask for.

THE ORACLE COVERS THE CODE AND THE SCHEMA. IT DOES NOT COVER THE PROSE, AND THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS PASS TREATED IT AS COVERING BOTH. `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.md` inlines every step sidecar and every front sidecar verbatim, and the deletion orphans prose in those sidecars. Correcting that prose CHANGES the projection, so a byte-exact criterion and a prose fix are mutually exclusive inside one commit. `docs/plans/agent-scaffold.success-criteria.md:20` states that the plan TOML "holds the Roadmap (step status, order, dependencies)", and it is a front sidecar, so correcting it fails a byte-exact criterion too.

THE PROSE CITES A STEP IN TWO SPELLINGS AND THE FIRST DRAFT SEARCHED ONE. Both are in the step sidecars:
