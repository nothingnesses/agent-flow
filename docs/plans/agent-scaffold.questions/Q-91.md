## Trade-offs

- `high` makes ordinary residual acceptance and ordinary narrowing unavailable while a high or critical finding remains unresolved. It fails closed on adoption and prevents a high finding from reaching delivery through an ordinary terminal shortcut. Its cost is that Q-78 supplies descriptive rather than prospective evidence and this floor removes choices that `critical` would retain.
- `critical` is the mandatory safety minimum. It blocks ordinary residual acceptance and narrowing for an unresolved critical, but permits the human to accept an unresolved high as residual risk. It offers more terminal flexibility at the cost of a weaker serious-finding boundary.
- `defer` would leave the proof without a selected delivery floor. A separate receipted floor decision would then be required before the proof could close or implementation could be planned.

## Judgement against the Project Principles

- **Make illegal states unrepresentable.** `high` removes ordinary delivery states for every unresolved high or critical rather than relying on a later actor to remember a policy exception.
- **Safe on existing projects.** The stricter floor fails closed when legacy adoption or incomplete state makes a serious finding uncertain.
- **Ground decisions in evidence.** The choice records the limit that Q-78 is descriptive, preserves both floors in the executable proof, and does not claim that the retained history proves the stricter floor is optimal.
- **Minimal by default.** `critical` would retain more terminal choices and `defer` would postpone proof work, but the selected `high` floor accepts that process cost to avoid a separate residual-high delivery state.
- **Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff.** One typed serious boundary across scope, dismissal, successor, terminal, and delivery states is cleaner than several local high-severity exceptions.
