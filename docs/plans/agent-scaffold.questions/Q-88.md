## Decision

The Q-86 proof-before-choice artefact reached its five-round cap with a clean streak of zero of two. Every round returned valid findings. The human chose how to close that capped artefact through the human-input contract.

The six presented options, in order, were:

1. `Narrow the proof scope`.
2. `Resume the full proof loop`.
3. `Fix, verify once, then close`.
4. `Accept the current synthesis`.
5. `Revert the synthesis`.
6. `Abandon the redesign`.

The recommendation was `Narrow the proof scope`. The human chose `Narrow the proof scope` on 2026-08-28.

## Trade-offs

- `Narrow the proof scope` ends the capped artefact, keeps all three architecture choices and their algebraic finite bounds, and moves the complete executable proof to a blocking gate for only the architecture the human later selects. It reduces wasted proof work while preserving the safety bar before implementation.
- `Resume the full proof loop` would reset the counters and continue proving all three candidates before choice. It preserves the original proof-before-choice ambition but spends more review and repair on two architectures the human will reject.
- `Fix, verify once, then close` would repair round 5 and close below the normal risky two-clean-round bar after one confirming review. It is cheaper than a reset but weakens the recorded convergence requirement and still asks one prototype to support all three options.
- `Accept the current synthesis` would end immediately but would accept four high, two medium, and three low valid round-5 findings, including unsound arbitrary-finite and cross-family safety claims.
- `Revert the synthesis` would remove the current options and bounds and return the question to exploration. It discards useful design work and the proof obligations exposed by five adversarial rounds.
- `Abandon the redesign` would stop further cost and leave the current workflow unchanged. It also leaves the known unbounded resume and acceptance paths without a planned replacement.

## Judgement against every Project Principle

- **Prefer the cleaner long-term architecture over the smallest diff.** A complete proof for the selected controller is cleaner than three broad prototype proofs with known unsound claims.
- **Minimal by default.** The choice avoids completing production-grade proofs for two architectures the human rejects.
- **Safe on existing projects.** The current workflow and code remain unchanged, and selected architecture implementation stays blocked on proof.
- **Idempotent.** The capped artefact retires with one durable decision rather than repeatedly resetting the same proof loop.
- **Make illegal states unrepresentable.** The selected-option proof gate gives a failed proof no legal path into implementation.
- **Ground decisions in evidence.** Five adversarial rounds show that the scripts expose obligations but do not establish controller safety.
- **Reproducible.** The scripts and all five triages remain durable evidence with explicit limitations, and the selected proof must reproduce and close each applicable finding.
- **Structured data first, project for humans.** The structured Q-88 question and exact JSONL receipt are the source of the decision, while Q-86, the synthesis, the owning step, and the generated plan are human projections.

## Fold

Q-88 is `decided -> folded into workflow-calibration`. The capped proof-before-choice counters retire. Q-86 stays `open`. The human still owns the architecture and serious terminal-floor choices. The later architecture choice authorises only the selected architecture's proof of concept, not production implementation. The later planner fold must materialise that proof as a separate reviewed Roadmap unit whose completion explicitly blocks every production implementation unit.
